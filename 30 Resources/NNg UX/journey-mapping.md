# Journey mapping [2015–17 core, 2026 notes]

Source: NN/g "Customer Journey Mapping" (Kaplan, 2017 London). Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]].

## Basics [2015–17, confirmed 2026]
A journey map visualises the process a person goes through to accomplish a goal over time. Five core elements: **actor** (persona or segment), **scenario and expectations**, **phases**, **actions, mindsets and emotions**, **opportunities** (with internal ownership and measures). A journey is a goal-driven end-to-end set of interactions; a touchpoint is a single interaction; the lifetime relationship is broader than either.

| Artifact | Point of view | Best for | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journey map | Individual as customer of one company | Touchpoint analysis, alignment, collaboration | Can be too abstract or overwhelming |
| Experience map | Human in a broader context, product-agnostic | Needs analysis, new-solution design | Lacks brand context |
| Service blueprint | Service process, front and back stage | Designing and fixing existing services | Needs cross-silo information |

Current vs future-state maps: current documents existing pain points; future/ideal state reinvents experience and differentiates.

## Process [2015–17]
1. Define scope and goal; engage a core stakeholder team; inventory existing research and touchpoints.
2. Build an assumption (hypothesis) map from internal views; identify research gaps.
3. Conduct qualitative research (interviews, direct observation, contextual inquiry, diary studies) to fill gaps; synthesise.
4. Document the evidence-based journey and prioritise opportunities with an action plan.
Shortcut: a 1–2 day stakeholder workshop yields a hypothesis map to validate later; weakness is it can be mistaken for final. Deep-research-first suits teams already committed.

**Prioritise actors and scenarios:** pick segments that matter to the bottom line, a mix of high-loyalty and high-risk actors, and scenarios with measurable impact (frequency of issue, transaction loss, critical content types, share of visitors affected).

## Pitfalls [2015–17]
Maps seen as "final", invented rather than researched data, design effort overwhelming content, no management buy-in, and mapping as the "program of the day": done well it anchors customer-centric transformation, done poorly it is abandoned.
