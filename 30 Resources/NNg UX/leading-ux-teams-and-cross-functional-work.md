# Leading UX teams and cross-functional work [2015–17 core, 2026 notes]

Sources: NN/g "Leading Highly-Effective UX Teams" (2016), "The UX VP/Director" (2016, 2017), "Working on Cross-Functional Teams" (2017). Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]]. Parallel product-leadership view: [[product-strategy-leading-teams-and-influence]].

## UX leadership [2015–17]
- Shift from tactical design lead to leader focused on influence, vision and business translation: tie UX to sales, cost and development time; companies don't hire for altruism toward the customer.
- Leader vs manager: both needed (leader: vision, disruption; manager: execution, process).
- Leadership styles (Goleman-type): commanding, visionary, democratic, coaching, pacesetting, affiliative, each with when-to-use and failure mode. Emotional intelligence and a compelling vision are the core traits.
- Understand business strategy: where does the firm aim to lead; where is strategy unclear (an opportunity to influence); negotiate minimum team size before joining.
- Maturity signals: a dedicated experience executive, UX directors who plan talent pipelines, broad skill mix, UX built into development processes, metrics.

## Org models [2015–17]
Fully centralised, centralised with assignments, matrixed (business-unit funded, dotted line), hybrid/federated, fully distributed. Trade-offs: central = global vision and standards; distributed = domain knowledge and agile fit. A single funding source helps efficiency. UX ideally reports at the same level as product and engineering ("a seat at the table"); direct reports 5–7; hire managers with experience in the function; first-time managers are best promoted internally. Define UX/dev responsibilities in writing; dedicate research recruiting and traffic roles in larger teams.

## Cross-functional teams [2015–17]
CFTs are often temporary, members at different levels reporting elsewhere; per the cited HBR piece many are dysfunctional. UX can only realise its best ideas there. Leader needs: collaborative leadership, managing varied experience, UX literacy (explain UX in their language, not design language). **Four decision levels** (facilitative leadership): decide and announce → gather input and decide → team input, boss decides → consensus with fallback; pick the level deliberately, explain why, and match to team experience. Communication-style assessments help.

## [2026] update
2025 NN/g guidance for UX leaders: accept evolving roles, bridge language gaps with stakeholders, balance user and business goals, build uniquely human skills (judgment, creativity, relationships), and move into strategy and systems thinking. With AI shortening build cycles, leaders must build shared judgment on what should be built, not just what can ([[ux-in-the-ai-era-2026]]).
