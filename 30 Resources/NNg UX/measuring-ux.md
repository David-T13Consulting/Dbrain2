# Measuring UX [2015–17 core]

Source: NN/g "Measuring User Experience" (Meyer, 2016–17 editions). Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]]. Related: [[ux-research-and-usability-testing]].

## Why and what
Quant testing answers "how many/how much" and supports: was the redesign worth it, does it beat a competitor or benchmark (e.g., a target success rate), what to prioritise, how to track across iterations. Qualitative answers "why/how to fix". Bad data → wrong picture → misplaced resources.

## Study design
Define questions first; choose within-subjects (same people, both designs; lower variability) vs between-subjects (mind assignment bias); keep conditions identical; recruit representative users; remove cheaters and outliers; pilot unmoderated tests (unclear instructions raise variance). Format: moderated vs remote unmoderated.

## Metrics
Success rate, time on task, error counts, satisfaction and difficulty ratings; ROI framing for executives. Sample size drives precision: a ±15% margin on success rate at 95% confidence needs roughly 39 data points (course figure).

## Statistics essentials
Report **confidence intervals** (average ± margin of error); correct reading: "we can be 95% confident the true value lies in this range", not a 95% probability statement. Wider with small samples and high variability; use geometric mean for task times (skewed). 

## PURE
Heuristic scorecard that quantifies ease of use cheaply: experts rate each step of key tasks; track across versions or against competitors. Useful when stakeholders want dashboards without full studies.

## [2026] note
Quant methods unchanged; AI tools can speed data cleaning and analysis but human review of statistics and study validity is still required ([[ux-research-and-usability-testing]]).
