# Personas [2015–17 core, 2026 notes]

Source: NN/g "Personas" (Laubheimer, 2016). Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]].

## Why [2015–17]
A persona is a fictional but fact-based representation of a cluster of target users. It stops teams designing for themselves, focuses decisions, keeps edge cases from dominating, and communicates users through stories (empathy needs narrative; "you can't have empathy for raw data"). Market segments (what, quantitative) are too general to design from; personas add the why.

## Choose the effort [2015–17]
Three types on a spectrum: **lightweight** (assumptive or proto-personas from a team workshop; cheap, fast, less credible), **qualitative** (research-based; the recommended core), **statistical** (large-sample validated). Do a situational analysis first: what drives the project, how the company is oriented, who will use the personas, what data you already have, and how big the scope is (one persona set vs per-role sets, as at a large health system where each project references only affected roles).

## Build [2015–17]
Get qualitative data (5–30 participants per role; trends appear from about 5), look for behaviour/attitude patterns rather than demographics, choose the most meaningful dimensions, group into segments (aim for 3–6 per effort), name and describe them with relevant detail only, validate with a larger sample if needed, then plan their use and revisit to prove ROI. Pitfalls: irrelevant colour, personas that feel fun rather than functional, no reinvestment and then no usage.

## [2026]
- NN/g guidance (Oct 2025): personas remain foundational when research-based and made collaboratively early; they also help recruit, guide expert reviews and segment analytics. Details must support design decisions.
- **Synthetic users and digital twins:** NN/g (Jun 2024) found AI-generated "users" optimistic, shallow and people-pleasing; fine for desk research, hypotheses and interview guides, not for decisions, niche populations or concept validation. Their Aug 2025 review of three studies found interview-based digital twins far more accurate than demographic personas (reported about 0.85 accuracy on survey tasks) while generic synthetic users show low variability; rich real interview data is the lever. Treat AI personas as a supplement to research, never a substitute. See [[ux-in-the-ai-era-2026]].
