# Persuasive design, trust and credibility [2015–17]

Source: NN/g "Persuasive Web Design" (2016–17). Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]]. Ethics caution applies; see [[#Ethics]]. Only the first half of the deck had extractable text.

## Framework
Persuasion works across stages of a visit (visit → stay → act) through trust and human-behaviour principles.

**Trust and credibility**
- Disposition to trust varies by person; trust has shifted from hierarchies to peers and credentialed experts.
- Boost credibility by lowering cognitive strain (simple words and layout read as more credible and insightful), featuring real people, free trials, localised content and testimonials, appropriate humour, honest negative reviews, and low initial commitment levels with transparent terms (hidden subscription terms destroy trust).
- Offer paths and user control, match the user's emotional state, and avoid suspicious interactions.
- **Measuring credibility:** guided studies (asking "rate trust factors") inflate importance; unguided tasks reveal real issues. Prominence-interpretation theory: impact = likelihood an element is noticed × meaning assigned. Practical: add confidence questions, ask "why", A/B test.

**Human behaviour:** System 1 (automatic) vs System 2 (effortful) thinking, so design for fast, effortless decisions; reciprocation and commitment/consistency principles; even a trivial "because" raises compliance.

## Ethics
Persuasion that exploits automatic thinking becomes manipulation (dark patterns). For regulated clients (banking, health) pair with consent, transparency and suitability duties.
