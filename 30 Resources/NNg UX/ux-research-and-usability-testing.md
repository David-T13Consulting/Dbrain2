# UX research methods and usability testing [2015–17 core, 2026 notes]

Sources: NN/g "UX Research Methods" (2017) and usability-testing material. Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]].

## Choosing a method [2015–17]
Three axes: **attitudinal vs behavioural** (what people say vs do), **qualitative (direct observation, why/how to fix) vs quantitative (indirect, how many/how much)**, and **context of product use** (natural, scripted, decontextualised, hybrid). Method map by stage:

| Stage | Question | Methods |
|---|---|---|
| Strategise | What shall we do? | Ethnography, competitive analysis, feature/task analysis, concept tests, segmentation |
| Optimise | How shall we do it? | Usability inspections, participatory design, iterative testing, desirability studies, lab usability studies |
| Assess | How well did we do? | Surveys (CSAT, NPS), UX assessments, benchmarks, analytics, A/B tests |

Principle: combine methods; each answers different questions.

## Interviews [2015–17]
Recruit representative participants; script but stay flexible; ask open, non-leading questions ("how useful would search be?" not "should it have search?"); avoid showing personal investment (politeness bias); acknowledge without judging; end with "anything I didn't ask?". Analyse by marking themes, coding into a spreadsheet to filter (not just count), or affinity diagramming.

## Usability testing [2015–17]
Qualitative (formative): small samples, 4–5 per group, find and fix problems. Quantitative (summative): 30–50 per group, benchmark or compare. Watch observer-expectancy bias (greater in qualitative). Tasks: derive from real user goals and language, keep simple and unambiguous, avoid external tools, top ~5 tasks; pilot-test.

## [2026] update
- NN/g: AI speeds up research **planning and analysis** (survey/interview drafting, transcription, preliminary coding, clustering, report drafting) but cannot observe behaviour; AI-moderated "testing" only sees transcripts and misses actions and nonverbal cues; AI interviewers handle structured feedback, not exploratory interviews. Treat AI like an intern: needs instructions, context and correction; humans own thematic analysis and judgment. (research-with-ai, reviewed Jan 2026.)
- Early gen-AI tools were studied qualitatively: see [[ux-in-the-ai-era-2026]].
- Principle that holds: what people do matters more than what they say.
