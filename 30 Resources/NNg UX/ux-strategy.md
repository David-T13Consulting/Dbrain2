# UX strategy [2015–17 core, 2026 notes]

Source: NN/g "User Experience Strategy" (2017 edition). Hub: [[30 Resources/NNg UX/README|NN/g hub]].

## Four flavours of strategy [2015–17]
| Flavour | Topics |
|---|---|
| Business | Porter's competitive strategies, innovation approaches, Business Model Canvas, brand strategy |
| Design/UX | Differentiation, delight, strategic design methods, research and insight |
| Process | Reusable design patterns, agile+UX, business metrics and review, spaces for creativity |
| Organisational | UX talent reality, org design, politics and ego, career development |

## Core ideas
- **Why UX matters:** customer experience is the competitive battleground (the "age of the customer"); UX's value has moved from usability to differentiation. Business model decides where UX can have impact, so map yours first.
- **Innovation = desirability ∩ feasibility ∩ viability** (design-thinking lenses): start from desirability, then check technology and business viability. Case: a holistic music-device experience; a phone launch that added large market value in two weeks (course figures, unverified).
- **Brand strategy → experience principles:** translate brand attributes into design principles and tone of voice, and test them (e.g., "conversational" fit a brand where "fun" offended).
- **Differentiation through delight**, but delight has a cost; destroying it by costs higher than expected is a risk.
- **Research dimensions:** attitudinal vs behavioural, qualitative vs quantitative, context of use; match method to stage (strategise / optimise / assess). See [[ux-research-and-usability-testing]].
- **Consistency as strategy:** design guidelines, patterns and visual architecture (grid, type, iconography) speed delivery and learnability; pitch pattern work in business terms (cost, revenue), as a pagination standardisation case did.
- **Process:** double diamond; classic agile ignores user-centred design; adaptations such as combined discover/define/design and Lean UX "think-make-check" cycles; "build" can mean code, prototype or concept.
- **Metrics:** execs love dashboards; use scorecards (PURE, see [[measuring-ux]]).
- **Org:** never enough UX talent; models range from fully centralised to fully distributed (trade-offs: global vision and standards vs domain knowledge and agile fit). See [[leading-ux-teams-and-cross-functional-work]].

## [2026] update
NN/g now frames UX strategy through AI: speed of AI-built features outpaces evaluation, so strategy must include embedding design knowledge in generation tools and fast evaluation capacity ([[ux-in-the-ai-era-2026]]). The 2025 "UX Reckoning" piece adds: tie UX to business outcomes, explain it in stakeholder language, and expand into systems thinking and strategy.

Use for David: the four-flavour map and the desirability/feasibility/viability lens complement [[product-strategy-vision-and-prioritisation]] for client product-strategy work.
