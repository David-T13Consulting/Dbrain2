# Product Strategy: leading teams and executive influence (modules 3–4)

Modules 3–4 of Product School **Product Strategy**. Hub: [[30 Resources/Product School/README|Product School hub]]. Previous: [[product-strategy-vision-and-prioritisation]]. Next: [[product-strategy-financials-and-ai-leadership]]. Own-words synthesis.

## Module 3: lead and develop high-performing teams

**Stop / start / continue.** Stop: solving problems yourself, writing specs to show you understand, being the loudest voice, measuring yourself by what you ship, attending every product meeting. Start: creating context others can execute against, building judgment in your PMs rather than giving answers, naming what good looks like before work begins, measuring yourself by what your team produces, protecting your team's time. Continue: staying close enough to know when someone needs help, caring about outcome not process, keeping product thinking sharp. "The habits that made you great as an IC will limit you here."

**Player-coach dial:** between over-player (bottleneck) and over-coach (abdication) sits a deliberate middle that shifts per person and per task: direct (new person, new task) → coach (growth edge) → support (complex ambiguity) → delegate (strength and experience).

**Performance conversations**
- Self-ownership before action: have I been clear? let it go too long? is my frustration about their behaviour or about a decision I made? am I doing this to help them or to relieve my discomfort?
- Diagnose first: repeated behaviour despite clear expectations = feedback problem; gap where expectations were never explicit = clarity problem; strong person blocked = system problem; gap persists despite feedback, clarity and support = hiring mismatch. Most "feedback problems" are clarity or system problems in disguise.
- Pitfalls: withholding (silence filled with the person's own story) and blurting (emotionally accurate, factually imprecise). Fix: **SBI**, Situation (specific, recent), Behaviour (observed, not interpreted), Impact, then an open Question.
- Five-step gap process: real-time SBI within 48 hours → state-of-the-relationship conversation → documented shared understanding → N-day check-in (date set in the conversation) → formal PIP as last step that should surprise no one.
- Recognise specifically and publicly; support development by giving scope before people ask. The strongest person is often most at risk because they absorb pressure silently; watch energy, response length and initiative.

**Creating conditions:** team structures are bets (outcome-oriented, feature-oriented, platform + product, small outcome team, each with a trade-off). The question is whether yours was chosen deliberately. Hiring: hire for the job you actually have; check direction, growth-stage (0→1, 1→10, 10→100), market, team and org ambiguity. **Psychological safety** = the condition that lets people raise problems early and admit mistakes; diagnose by when someone last disagreed with you, admitted a mistake early, or challenged a peer without you facilitating. **Team charter** (test: could a new PM on day one know what to do?): what we own and don't, how we decide (with escalation timing), what success looks like (numbers), how we work (concrete norms).

## Module 4: drive alignment and executive influence

**Stakeholders:** the org chart is not the power map. Formal power (title, budget, sign-off) tells you who must say yes; informal power (trust, history, relationships) tells you whose support to earn beforehand. Map by influence × agreement: blockers (find what they protect), sponsors (give them your narrative and specific ask), validators (channel their signal upward), noise (acknowledge briefly). Tips: find who influences the person; understand concerns that may not be public. Resistance types: logical (needs evidence), interest (show convergence with their goals), identity (frame as the next chapter, not a criticism of their work). **Five whys** to diagnose a stall; the fifth usually exposes a framing problem (we built the case around our priorities, not theirs).

**Hard calls:** whose work gets cut, when to overrule, when you are overruled and must back it. What sharpens a call: organisation precedent, relationships, business trade-offs plus your accumulated judgment. Truths: your team reads your conviction not your doubts; you are further from the decision as you get senior; the goal is owning the outcome. Saying no that holds: lead with what stays open, state plainly, firm on the call but generous with the person, offer the path forward in the same breath; test: could the person repeat this back as a clear no?

**Negotiation:** lead with interest, not position (position is zero-sum), and know your BATNA. Sequence: come prepared (need vs ask, BATNA) → open by observing, then ask → state disagreements clearly (rarely the surface issue) → if stuck, invite new options and fall back on interests. **Executive narrative** = recommendation + trade-off (what the bet gives up) + reason to trust it. Use **BLUF** (bottom line up front); deliver as a one-page pre-read plus a 3-minute ask. Takeaways: alignment is won before the room; hard calls are sharpened by weighing the cost of your instinct, not by more data.

Use for David: stakeholder map, five whys and BLUF are directly reusable in client steering-committee preparation.
