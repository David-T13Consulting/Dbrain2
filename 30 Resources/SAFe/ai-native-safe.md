# AI-Native SAFe

Scaled Agile's **AI-Native SAFe** (announced 16 June 2026, released 23 June 2026, fully unveiled at
the SAFe & AI Summit San Diego, 14–18 September 2026). This page covers what it is, what changes
compared with Core SAFe 6.0, what is confirmed and what is still interpretation, and what it means for
David's three roles.

**Classification `internal`.** Sections 1–17 are built only from published web sources retrieved
1 October 2026, with no toolkit material. Section 18 covers David's own positioning, which is why the
page is not `public`. The release is about three months old, so treat this page as a moving target.

## 1. What it is, in one paragraph

AI-Native SAFe is a **second operating model** that sits alongside Core SAFe. It is not a version
number. It redesigns teams, ARTs, events, portfolio governance and the delivery pipeline for
organisations where AI generates much of the output. Scaled Agile frames the thesis as: **"The
bottleneck has moved."** Building is no longer the constraint. The constraint is **validating whether
what is built is safe, secure and valuable**, and steering by **outcomes rather than outputs** [A].
Scaled Agile calls it "the biggest evolution to date" and an operating model "built to enable
organizations to maximize value and returns from AI with proven Lean-Agile governance for the AI era" [A].

## 4. The case for change

- **AI-Empowered Agility** [A] is defined as "the capability to rapidly develop and responsibly deploy
  AI-driven products and solutions whilst also leveraging AI to continuously enhance the speed, quality,
  and adaptability of existing Lean-Agile methods".
- About **two-thirds of companies are stuck in isolated AI pilots** that fail to scale. The
  "Future-Built" top 5% have re-architected around agentic and generative AI [A].
- **Only 9% of C-suite executives** report significant value from generative AI despite record
  investment [A, summit press release].
- Treating AI as a one-off initiative "leads to fragmented data, tech debt, and workflows" [A].
- Organisations with mature **Lean Portfolio Management** are reported to measure AI investment
  returns better: hypothesis discipline transfers to AI governance [B].

## 5. The four Lean-Agile shifts for the age of AI

These are the conceptual spine of AI-Native SAFe [A]. Each shift carries three of the **twelve
critical success factors** used in the "Launching AI-Native ARTs" course [B].

| # | Shift: from → to | What it means | Critical success factors (as reported) [C] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outcomes → outcomes and intent** (flow optimised for output volume → **outcome-driven flow**) | Steer by objectively measurable value and declared intent | OKR alignment · validated data strategy · business-outcome dashboards |
| 2 | **Cross-functional teams → AI-augmented cross-functional teams** | AI agents as teammates with defined accountability | AI Value Architects · AI governance and ethics policies at ART level · *(third factor not captured)* |
| 3 | **Iterative learning → rapid experimentation** (accelerated PDCA) | AI compresses build and analysis time, so learning cadence becomes the constraint | Updated product vision · customer demos · outcome-focused roadmaps |
| 4 | **Development at scale → development *and innovation* at scale** | Coordinate discovery across ARTs and portfolios, not just delivery | Design and context repositories · AI innovation pipeline investment · governance automation |

**Sustaining element: a human-centric AI culture** [A]. AI is human augmentation, which frees people
for strategy, creativity and ethical judgment. "Human judgment remains the final, non-negotiable loop
for value, safety, and purpose." Human-in-the-loop (HITL) means people verify AI output, assure quality
and remove bias, and human effort shifts to orchestration and critical thinking [C].

> ⚠ The CSF grouping above comes from a translated secondary source. It lists 11 named factors, and
> its grouping doesn't map cleanly onto the shifts (e.g. "customer demos" sits under rapid
> experimentation). Use it as indicative only, until the "Launching AI-Native ARTs" course materials
> are checked.

## 6. Outcomes over outputs (Session 2)

### The outcome-driven product development cycle [A]

Applied at team, ART and portfolio level:

1. **Outcomes**: expressed as **OKRs**, from portfolio strategy contributions down to team-level
   PI intentions.
2. **Priorities**: portfolios direct funding; ARTs and teams commit capacity.
3. **Outputs**: products and solutions at portfolio level; **features, prototypes, enablers and
   experiments** at ART and team level.
4. **Measurements**: continuous monitoring to identify the winning outputs and pivot quickly away from
   underperformers.
5. **Value**: customer outcomes achieved, and the business results that follow.

### Outcome hierarchy [A]

Portfolio outcomes → **ART outcomes** → **PI outcomes** → **team outcomes**, all expressed as OKRs.
ART outcomes become **active planning inputs** rather than retrospective summaries. They provide
cross-ART alignment, context for teams, and baselines for Sense and Respond.

*[C] Agility at Scale describes this as **outcome trees** with bidirectional traceability, plus a
**moonshot vs roofshot** ambition label so that missed stretch objectives aren't misread. It attributes
outcome trees to the Kersten session. The term doesn't appear in the [A] Session 2 summary, so verify it.*

### Product vision and roadmaps [A]

- **Product vision pillars:** **data-informed** (AI sentiment analysis, ML-driven real-time market
  synthesis), **strategy-aligned** (portfolio outcome boundaries, financial guardrails),
  **technology-anchored** (architectural viability).
- **Outcome-driven roadmaps** visualise intent, milestones, releases and **incremental key results**
  rather than lists of outputs.

### Investment strategy changes [A]

- **Quarterly** reallocation of resources, replacing 6–9 month annual planning cycles.
- **Many small bets**, fail fast.
- **Fixed and variable costs tracked separately**, because AI compute is a variable operating cost.
- **Token-consumption guardrails** as a budget control.

Reference case cited: **Cognition (Devin)**. They release features and agents weekly, and leadership
filters outputs against strategic outcomes and discards misaligned work [A]. Related book: Mik Kersten,
*Output to Outcome* (July 2026), on the risk that "AI can endlessly amplify outputs" [A].

## 7. Intent, specifications, context, and curated data

**The information stack** [A]. For an AI partner to be reliable it needs three connected elements:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| **Intent** | *Why*: the outcome sought |
| **Specifications** | *What / how* |
| **Context** | *Where*: policies, goals, domain |

When these are unified in a shared information stack, agents can retrieve the context, policies and
goals they need without a human re-briefing them each time.

**Curated data** [A] is deliberately constructed data that is **complete, accurate, maintained,
securely accessed**, and **built on the architectural runway ahead of need**.

- [B] calls curated data "the single biggest predictor of AI success" and stresses fit-for-purpose
  datasets per use case over data-lake approaches.
- [C] says the model moves data curation from an invisible team-level cost to a portfolio-funded
  operating decision.

**Secondary vocabulary** [C], not confirmed in Scaled Agile primary text:

- **Intent declaration**: "teams stop writing 'add AI' and start declaring the intent of each AI
  system", for example "reduce decision latency on portfolio investments". No AI system enters the
  backlog without one.
- **Action-boundary specification**: explicit limits on which operations, systems and decision
  thresholds an agent may cross. Agility at Scale borrows this from the **IMDA Agentic AI Governance
  Framework** (Singapore, May 2026). It is not a SAFe term.
- **Designed human–AI handoffs**: where work passes to AI, the quality checks the output must clear,
  and who owns the result [C, Efficiently Connected]. The press coverage of "explicit handoffs" [A] supports
  the concept.

## 8. AI-Native ARTs and teams (Session 3)

### Six characteristics of an AI-Native ART [A]

1. **Organised around products**: a single operational product, or a group of related customer-facing products.
2. **Outcome-driven**: measured by "customer and business outcomes achieved rather than the volume of outputs".
3. **Powered by human expertise and judgment**: AI executes; "people bring the product and domain
   expertise and remain accountable for all key decisions".
4. **Balancing rapid innovation with cadence-based learning**: cadence stops local decisions and
   dependencies from piling up unchecked.
5. **Optimising shared AI workflows and platforms**: improvements are built once into the shared
   **Continuous Innovation and Delivery Pipeline (CIDP)** instead of being solved locally again and again.
6. **Ensuring AI governance and ethics**: ART-level guardrails for safe, ethical AI use.
   [B] lists four supporting practices: define acceptable-risk policies, integrate governance into
   shared platforms, set standards for consistency, operationalise human accountability. As
   [B] puts it: *"Agents require management. People require leadership."*

**Smaller teams** and **shorter, more iterative cycles** are part of the headline change [A, press release].

### AI-Native teams: capabilities instead of roles [A]

| Capability | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Product** | Defines the *why*; aligns to the product vision; synthesises customer feedback |
| **Builder** | Uses AI to generate outputs aligned to outcomes |
| **Domain expert** | Supplies customer and business context that turns generic AI proposals into fit-for-purpose solutions |
| **AI** | Handles "tedious, repetitive tasks within clear guidelines", under human supervision |

**Team workflow = Align → Sense → Respond** [A]:

- **Align**: critical decisions and outcome alignment.
- **Sense**: analyse feedback, metrics and learning from several sources.
- **Respond**: adjust workflows and product direction based on what was sensed.

[B] reports **no prescribed team-level iteration boundaries**: teams choose their own interaction
patterns. ART-level cadence still exists, since Sense and Respond runs every iteration. [B] also points
out that the **Scrum Master / Team Coach is not mentioned** in the team or ART guidance, and asks who
will help teams design their interaction patterns. This is an open gap.

## 9. Roles

### New: AI Value Architect [A]

Introduced because organisations struggle to get tangible value from AI. It has five responsibilities:

| # | Responsibility | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Coaching AI adoption** | Adoption treated as "a coaching discipline rather than a training exercise"; builds fluency in the teams it supports |
| 2 | **Unlocking value from AI workflows and tools** | Gets value from AI features already in existing platforms before building anything new |
| 3 | **Bridging business and technology** | Works across functions on "risk, legal, data, and ethical" considerations and treats them as business opportunities |
| 4 | **Facilitating AI solution development** | Handles "new failure modes" (data quality shifts, model drift, evaluation gaps) and challenges dangerous assumptions |
| 5 | **Optimising outcomes** | "Relentless focus" on business and customer outcomes |

- **Scope:** coaches **several teams and solutions**. The focus is AI adoption, not team-level agility
  or flow [B].
- **Candidate pools:** a "potential career path for SAFe Scrum Masters and SPCs" [B]. The course
  targets consultants and coaches, product, program and delivery leaders, Scrum Masters, senior
  sponsors, solution architects and program managers [B].
- **Course:** "AI Value Architect", released 25 Aug 2026. Two days live plus ~2 hours on demand. No
  SAFe or technical prerequisite. "AI-Native Foundations" is highly recommended. Topics include the role,
  coaching adoption, unlocking value from existing workflows, **building a functioning multi-agent AI
  solution**, bridging business and technology, **designing the Value Blueprint**, tracking delivery and
  scaling adoption [B].
- *[C] places it "at the portfolio–solution intersection", owning cost, ethics, legal exposure and risk
  decisions. The press release also mentions cost, ethics, legal and risk [A], so the gist holds.*

### What happens to Core SAFe roles

| Core SAFe role | AI-Native status | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| **RTE** | Stays. Facilitates **ART Sense and Respond**; manages variability and the deliberate discarding of work | [A] |
| **Product Management / PO** | Not detailed as roles. Content authority moves into the team **product capability** plus ART/PI outcome ownership | [A] capabilities; mapping is interpretation |
| **Scrum Master / Team Coach** | **Not mentioned** in the ART/team guidance; seen as a candidate pool for AI Value Architect | [B] |
| **System Architect / Enterprise Architect** | EAs co-own portfolio AI governance; architecture keeps the runway, including runway for curated data | [A] |
| **Business Owners / LPM** | Portfolio leaders, the VMO, finance executives and EAs establish AI governance | [A] |
| **SPC / ASPC / SPCT** | Upgrade paths; SPCs train via "Implementing AI-Native SAFe"; ASPC listed in roadmap phase 5 | [A] upgrade; [C] roadmap detail |
| New governance bodies | **AI Council** (corporate AI policy: permitted, prohibited, supervised; ethical, legal and financial risk) and **AI Center of Excellence** (high-value use cases, no duplicated solutions, shared standards and tools) | [C] |

## 10. Events (Session 4)

| Core SAFe 6.0 | AI-Native SAFe | Cadence / duration | Facilitator | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PI Planning (2 days) | **PI Outcome Planning** | Once per PI · **1 day** | (RTE implied) | [A] |
| Inspect and Adapt (end of PI) | **ART Sense and Respond** | **Every iteration · 2 hours** | **RTE** | [A] |
| System Demo (every iteration) | **Customer Demos** (plus demo content absorbed into Sense and Respond) | "Held regularly" | — | [A] demos; [B] "replaces System Demo" |

> ⚠ Conflict: an early [B] note (18 Jun) described Sense and Respond as an **end-of-PI** analytics
> event. The later [A] Session 4 blog (5 Aug) says **every iteration, two hours**. Use [A].

### PI Outcome Planning [A]

**Purpose:** align and commit to the **PI outcomes** that best advance the ART's long-term strategy;
derive **team outcomes**; identify executable milestones. **Participants:** everyone on the AI-Native
ART. **Key artifact:** the **PI Outcome Planning Board**, showing how PI outcomes are realised through
team outcomes and the milestones the ART commits to.

**Agenda (one day):**

1. **Business context**: leadership shares the product vision, ART outcomes and *draft* PI outcomes.
2. **Outcome breakouts**: cross-team representatives plan how to deliver a specific PI outcome.
   *(New. This is the structural change: planning is organised around outcomes, not around teams.)*
3. **Team breakouts**: each team balances its commitments across the PI outcomes.
4. **Draft plan presentation**.
5. **Management review and problem solving**.
6. **Planning adjustments**.
7. **Second breakout cycle**.
8. **Final plan presentation**.
9. **Commitment vote**.

**Compared with Core PI Planning:** the same skeleton (context → breakouts → draft → management
review → adjust → final → vote) compressed into one day. The planning object changes from **features
and PI objectives** to **outcomes and milestones**, and outcome breakouts are added.

### ART Sense and Respond [A]

- **Purpose:** "managing variability"; **intentionally discarding ineffective work**; sharing ART-level
  systemic learning.
- **Structure:**
  - First half: **four sensing activities**.
  - Second half: **three response activities**.
  - *(The individual activities are not named in the public summary.)*
- **Three perspectives:**
  1. **Objective outcome progress**.
  2. **Customer feedback** (metrics plus direct demos).
  3. **Workflow improvement** prioritised on evidence.
- **Effect:** learning is "pulled forward from the end of the PI into every iteration" [C]. The I&A
  problem-solving workshop has no explicit equivalent in the public material. Treat that as a gap.

### Customer Demos [A]

Demos run with customers directly to validate desirability and real-world use. They are chosen on two
dimensions:

- **Purpose:** learning-driven vs persuasion-focused.
- **Reach:** large group vs personal.

Rationale: "AI accelerates product development, but using AI without collaborative customer feedback
risks building flawed products."

## 11. Continuous Innovation and Delivery Pipeline / AI innovation pipeline (Session 5)

The Core SAFe **Continuous Delivery Pipeline** (CE → CI → CD → RoD) is extended into the **Continuous
Innovation and Delivery Pipeline (CIDP)**. It spans discovery through **go-to-market** [A for the
name; B for "discovery through GTM"].

The **AI innovation pipeline** is designed to "handle high volumes of AI outputs and automated flows"
while "accelerating learning and ideation by continuously delivering validated solutions" [A]. It has
four components:

| Component | Definition (quoted or close paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| **AI-empowered workflows** | Collaboration between people and agents moving outputs through the lifecycle |
| **Embedded policies** | "Specify the boundaries, approvals, and stopping rules that keep work safe and ensure accountability for agents and people alike" |
| **Insights and evidence** | "Capture the pipeline's audit trail of decisions, actions, and data, and the insights it reveals" |
| **Shared platforms** | "Provide a shared technical foundation that organizes, links, and expands the workflows, policies, insights, evidence" |

**Work items flowing through it:** prototypes, experiments, features, enablers [A].

**Go-to-market inside the ART** [A]. GTM is treated as an integrated system with two parts:

- a **durable GTM strategy**: market reach and customer priority;
- **recurring GTM cycles** covering **value activation**, **adoption enablement** and **outcome
  measurement**.

Product marketing, sales enablement and customer success are **embedded in AI-Native ARTs**.

## 12. AI governance and ethics (Session 5)

**Who:** portfolio leaders, the **VMO**, finance executives and enterprise architects. It operates
"continuously across value streams" [A].

| Pillar | Mechanisms [A] |
|---|---|
| **1. Controlling AI spend** | Track AI spend and usage-based **token costs**; operating-cost guardrails; **token limits by product and role** |
| **2. Enforcing responsible AI** | Keep AI "trustworthy, explainable, and compliant"; respond to emerging AI law; **human-centric controls via formal checkpoints in portfolio reviews** |
| **3. Monitoring agentic behaviours** | Automated checks plus human review; multi-layer oversight to prevent "infinite recursive loops" and local workflows that "break across broader product contexts" |
| **4. Managing and exploiting AI risk** | Systems thinking that turns "risk controls into competitive advantages"; **mandatory AI evaluations aggregated into shared portfolio libraries** |

**AI ethics foundation** [A]: the "moral compass for decision-making", with "core organizational values
embedded directly into curated data and context", so that teams ask "**Should we?**" as well as
"**Could we?**".

**Measurement** [C]: CALMR is presented as governing AI-augmented delivery, tracked through lower
deployment risk, shorter lead time for fixes, lower defect severity and better MTTR. **Assessments**
[C]: an "AI-Empowered" assessment per SAFe Discipline in Comparative Agility, with a **SAFe CoPilot**
that generates next steps.

**External frameworks it maps to** (my synthesis, for regulated clients):

| External regime | Where it plugs into AI-Native SAFe |
|---|---|
| **EU AI Act** (risk classes, high-risk obligations, transparency) | Pillar 2 checkpoints; embedded policies in the pipeline; evaluation libraries as evidence |
| **NIST AI RMF** (Govern / Map / Measure / Manage) | Governance body = Govern; intent and context = Map; evaluations and CALMR = Measure; spend, agent monitoring and risk = Manage |
| **ISO/IEC 42001** (AI management system) | The portfolio governance structure plus the pipeline audit trail as AIMS evidence |
| **SR 11-7 / model risk** (US banks) | Model validation as mandatory evaluations; drift monitoring under "new failure modes" |
| **MDR / IVDR + AI Act** (medtech) | Curated data and evidence trails toward technical documentation; HITL as the human oversight requirement |

*(Mapping is interpretation, to verify per client. See [[regulated-environments]].)*

## 13. Five AI value patterns

Attributed to Andrew Sales's "Architecting for the Future" keynote at SAFe Summit Amsterdam 2026.
Also taught in the "Leading an AI-Native Organization" course as patterns "that separate AI Natives
from AI Tourists" [C, corroborated by B].

| Pattern | Mechanism | Typical layer | What to instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge and decision support** | Compress information into decision-ready context | Portfolio / solution | Decision quality and speed; evidence freshness |
| **Customer interaction** | Scale availability and consistency with demand | ART / team | Interaction outcomes; escalation rate; trust signals |
| **Workforce automation** | AI executes, with human exception paths | Solution / ART | Throughput shift; exception rate; capacity redeployed |
| **Risk and control** | Continuous monitoring replaces periodic sampling | Portfolio | Control coverage; audit evidence; detection time |
| **Expert productivity** | Scarce specialists leveraged through AI pairing | Team | Expert throughput; review quality; flow |

Useful as a **portfolio triage lens**: map each value stream to its dominant pattern before funding AI
work [C].

| Phase | Purpose [A] | Activities and accelerators [C] | Outputs [C] |

**Choosing the first AI-Native ART** [C]:

1. Leadership visibly invested in AI's potential.
2. Well-defined products with measurable outcomes.
3. Teams already adopting AI tools.
4. A real AI opportunity with direct impact.

Compare the Core SAFe criteria (leadership support, collaborating teams, clear product, significant
challenge). See [[transformation-playbook]].

**For Core SAFe organisations:** revisit the greyed-out phases 1–2, especially **AI governance and
ethics**, and check that ARTs are organised around **products** [B].

## 16. Core SAFe 6.0 vs AI-Native SAFe at a glance

| Dimension | Core SAFe 6.0 | AI-Native SAFe |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | Deliver value in the shortest sustainable lead time | **The bottleneck has moved**: validate value, safety and security; outcomes over outputs |
| Steering object | Features, PI objectives (business value 1–10), flow metrics | **Outcomes (OKRs)** at portfolio, ART, PI and team level; outcome-driven roadmaps |
| Team | Cross-functional Agile team (PO, SM/TC, developers), 5–11 people | **Smaller AI-augmented teams** built on capabilities (product, builder, domain expert, AI); Align–Sense–Respond |
| ART | Organised around value streams | Organised around **products**; six characteristics, incl. shared AI platforms and governance |
| Planning | PI Planning, 2 days | **PI Outcome Planning**, 1 day, with outcome breakouts |
| Review / improve | System Demo each iteration; I&A each PI | **Sense and Respond** each iteration (2h); **Customer Demos** |
| Pipeline | Continuous Delivery Pipeline | **Continuous Innovation and Delivery Pipeline**; AI innovation pipeline with embedded policies and an audit trail; GTM inside the ART |
| Funding | Lean budgets, guardrails, participatory budgeting ~twice a year | **Quarterly** reallocation; small bets; fixed vs variable cost; **token guardrails** |
| Governance | Lean governance; compliance in flow | Portfolio **AI governance and ethics** (spend, responsible AI, agentic monitoring, AI risk); AI Council / CoE |
| New role | — | **AI Value Architect** |
| Data | Not a top-level construct | **Curated data** is a framework-level element, on the architectural runway |

## 17. Critique and open questions

| Issue | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Unproven** | The release is weeks to months old. No organisation has run it for enough PIs to show whether it **absorbs** coordination overhead (AI summarising and assembling decision support) or **amplifies** it (new elements on top of the existing cadence) | [C] |
| **Overhead legacy** | Core SAFe is already criticised as demanding and expensive. Every new element has to "pay rent" in coordination saved or risk reduced | [C] |
| **"Acceleration without groundwork"** | The likely failure mode. AI "accelerates the system you already have" and amplifies dysfunction. Do the groundwork first: curated data, handoff design, team norms for AI use, governance mapping, measurement baseline | [C] |
| **Team coaching gap** | SM/TC absent from the guidance; who coaches teams in designing their own interaction patterns? | [B] |
| **Trust erosion** | HBR research (2026) cited: AI in team workflows can erode trust, narrow participation and cause "ownership drift". Re-anchor decisions as team property | [C] |
| **Curriculum decay** | AI course content goes stale quickly | [B] |
| **Vendor strategy** | High switching costs turn SAFe's incumbency into a governance extension. Competitors include McKinsey, BCG and the SIs | [C] |
| **Not specified publicly** | Names of Sense and Respond's 4+3 activities; the full 12 CSFs; AI-Native portfolio events; how PI outcomes are scored (does predictability survive?); team size; whether PI length changes | Gap |

**Suggested falsifiable adoption test** [C, author's proposal, not official]. Before adopting, write down
which metrics must move, by how much, within **two PIs**. If they don't move, the path back to Core SAFe
stays open.

## 18. What this means for David

**Product Leader.** AI-Native SAFe is Scaled Agile catching up with outcome-based product management:
OKR hierarchies, outcome roadmaps, small bets, customer demos, GTM inside the ART. Your existing
vocabulary (hypotheses, leading indicators, discovery before delivery) is now SAFe-native. The **intent,
specifications and context** stack and **curated data** match how agentic products are actually built.

**Program Manager.** The RTE job becomes **variability management**: run a two-hour Sense and Respond
every iteration and deliberately kill work. That replaces the PI-end I&A rhythm. Outcome breakouts in a
one-day PI Outcome Planning demand much better pre-work than feature-based planning: draft PI outcomes,
data baselines, dependency views.

**Transformation Consultant.** Most clients are still on Core SAFe 6.0 or earlier, and many are in the
"two-thirds stuck in pilots" bucket. That makes the **governance and ethics pillars**, **token/spend
guardrails** and **evaluation libraries** the entry point, rather than a framework swap. In banking,
asset management and medtech, mapping AI-Native governance onto the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001,
SR 11-7 and MDR (section 12) is a differentiated offer few SAFe coaches can make.

**Possible moves** (options, not recommendations):

1. **AI Value Architect** is the AI-Native role closest to David's positioning (regulated-industry
   operator-consultant, AI product governance, evals). The course has no SAFe prerequisite. Trade-off:
   a vendor credential in a fast-moving field, against a clear label that US and EU buyers of SAFe will
   recognise.
2. The **AI Second Brain** evaluation and governance layer is a working small-scale example of
   "insights and evidence", "embedded policies" and "mandatory evaluations aggregated into libraries".
   It could serve as a live demo of these concepts.
3. Credentials: confirm whether David's earlier SAFe certifications are still active. The free
   AI-Native Upgrade requires active membership.
