# Lean-Agile leadership, culture and organisational agility

The leadership side of SAFe. It covers three of the seven 6.0 core competencies (Lean-Agile
Leadership, Continuous Learning Culture, Organisational Agility), which are now grouped as the
**Leadership and Culture** discipline **[2024+]**. Built from the Executive Workshop, Implementation
Roadmap and role-mapping toolkits.

## Why leaders first

> "It is not enough that management commit themselves to quality and productivity, they must know what
> it is they must do. Such a responsibility cannot be delegated." — Deming, quoted throughout the toolkits

The Implementation Roadmap trains executives and managers *before* practitioners. In practice, a
transformation where leaders delegate SAFe to "the agile team" is the most reliable predictor of
failure.

## Lean-Agile Leadership: three dimensions

| Dimension | What it looks like | Anti-signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Mindset, values and principles** | Leaders can explain the House of Lean, the Agile Manifesto and the 10 principles *in their own business terms* | Leaders treat SAFe as an IT process |
| **Leading by example** | Authenticity, emotional intelligence, lifelong learning, developing people, decentralised decisions | "Do as I say": leaders still demand Gantt charts and fixed-scope dates |
| **Leading change** | Change vision, a guiding coalition, psychological safety, transformation leadership | Change is announced, then left to coaches |

**Mindset shift for every new role** (from the role-mapping deck):

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Coordinating | Coaching and collaborating |
| Setting deadlines | Setting objectives and explaining the *why* |
| Detailed, specific outcomes | Investing in the group's overall performance |
| Having all the answers | Helping the team find answers |
| Directing | Letting self-organising, self-managing teams emerge |

## Leading change: Kotter's eight steps, mapped to the Implementation Roadmap

| Kotter step | Roadmap step(s) | SAFe mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Create a sense of urgency | Reaching the tipping point | Burning platform *or* proactive leadership; 3-minute pitch |
| 2. Build a guiding coalition | Train change agents (SPCs); train execs/managers; create the LACE | LACE, Leading SAFe |
| 3. Form a strategic vision | Identify value streams and ARTs; create the implementation plan | VS/ART identification workshop |
| 4. Enlist a volunteer army | Prepare for ART launch | Role mapping (pull/hybrid), training |
| 5. Remove barriers | Train teams and launch the ART | PI Planning, management review and problem solving |
| 6. Generate short-term wins | Coach ART execution | System Demos, predictability, I&A |
| 7. Sustain acceleration | Launch more ARTs and value streams | "Rinse and repeat" |
| 8. Institute change | Extend to the portfolio; accelerate | LPM, APMO, CoPs, Measure and Grow |

**Two reasons to change** (Implementation Roadmap):

1. **Burning platform.** The company is failing to compete and the current way of working won't
   produce a solution in time.
2. **Proactive leadership.** With no burning platform, leaders must *create* urgency by taking a
   stand for a better future state. This is harder, and it is the more common case for profitable banks
   and asset managers.

## Management's role in SAFe

Managers are not removed. Their job changes:

1. **Lead the mission.** Minimal-constraint mission; act as Business Owners; sit on the APMO or LACE;
   collaborative strategy; eliminate impediments.
2. **Develop people.** Coaching, careers, compensation, performance; remove demotivating policies;
   unlock intrinsic motivation; honest feedback and recognition.
3. **Act as lean-thinking manager-teachers.** Reinforce the values, support the principles, drive
   Lean-Agile excellence, lead the transformation.

## Continuous Learning Culture

| Dimension | Content |
|---|---|
| **Learning organisation** | Senge's five disciplines: *personal mastery* (T-shaped skills), *shared vision*, *team learning*, *mental models*, *systems thinking* |
| **Innovation culture** | Innovative people; time and space for innovation (IP iteration, hackathons); Go and See (Gemba); experimentation and feedback; **pivot without mercy or guilt**; **innovation riptides** (continuous portfolio flow of innovation epics) |
| **Relentless improvement** | A constant sense of danger; optimise the whole; problem-solving culture; reflect at key milestones; fact-based improvement |

## Organisational Agility

| Dimension | Content |
|---|---|
| **Lean-thinking people and Agile teams** | Lean and Agile practices spread *beyond IT*: HR, finance, legal, marketing |
| **Lean business operations** | Map the operational value stream; measure **process time vs delay time** (the Executive Workshop example: 47-day lead time, a fraction of it actual work); remove delays |
| **Strategy agility** | Continuous market sensing; decisive strategy changes; organise and reorganise around value; innovate like a start-up; implement quickly; **ignore sunk costs** |

## Diagnostic: "without" symptoms per competency

From the Executive Workshop's Business Agility diagnostic. Use it with a leadership team: everyone
counts the symptoms they recognise per competency, then compare the counts.

| Competency | Symptoms when it is missing |
|---|---|
| **Lean-Agile Leadership** | Mindset reinforces the status quo; leaders say one thing and do another; no safe experimentation; constant escalation → long lead times; Agile applied inconsistently |
| **Team and Technical Agility** | Silo dependencies slow delivery; even Agile teams can't deliver independently; priorities misaligned; endemic quality issues; non-Agile business teams slow things down |
| **Agile Product Delivery** | Cross-team coordination too complicated; planning slow and unrealistic; feedback too late; big infrequent risky releases; customers don't love the product |
| **Enterprise Solution Delivery** | No idea how to be agile on large systems; waterfall suppliers don't fit; integration too late; compliance overhead slows delivery; systems not designed to evolve after deployment |
| **Lean Portfolio Management** | Strategy unclear to those executing it; project funding causes friction and negotiation and kills innovation; inconsistent practices; output metrics, not outcomes; governance blocks flow |
| **Organisational Agility** | Can't reorganise for new strategy; people practices reward individuals over teams; end-to-end operations ill-defined; customer and market needs discovered too late; slow to adapt |
| **Continuous Learning Culture** | Knowledge not shared; mistakes repeated; less innovative than before; no time to learn or experiment; symptoms fixed instead of root causes |

## Accelerators in the toolkit archive

- **SAFe Executive Workshop** (deck + workbook, A4 and Letter): a half-day executive session covering
  digital-age disruption (Perez's technological revolutions), the dual operating system, the seven
  competencies and their symptoms, next steps and the three-minute pitch. Usually followed by Leading SAFe.
- **Introducing SAFe** deck and *Achieving Business Agility with SAFe 5* white paper: overview material.
- **SAFe customer stories** deck: case evidence for executive conversations *(dated: 5.1 era)*.

## Use it as…

- **TC (primary):** open every executive engagement with the "without" diagnostic and the dual
  operating system. Both are framed in business symptoms, not SAFe terms, which suits banking and
  healthcare executives. Make "managers first" a contract condition of the engagement.
- **PL:** your leadership credibility in SAFe comes from strategy agility. Show you can *stop* things
  (ignore sunk costs; pivot without guilt).
- **PgM:** you lead by example in every sync. Model decentralised decision-making and avoid becoming
  the escalation hub.
