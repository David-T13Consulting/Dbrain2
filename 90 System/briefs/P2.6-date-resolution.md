# Date resolution that follows how people actually speak

## Goal (one sentence)
The phone resolves the date a person types or says (English and French) to the right calendar day, or says plainly that it cannot, without a hand-written phrase list that needs a new review round for every new wording.

## Why
- MVP-phone review rounds 5–14 each found one more wording the hand-built parser mishandled. A phrase list does not converge, and the stuck rule (D74) already pointed to a redesign.
- David chose Q72 (a) on 7 Oct: ship the supported forms only. Everything else asks again. This brief is the replacement for the "everything else" part.
- Voice (Q50, step A) will bring spoken phrasing ("a week from Thursday", "on the first", "the Friday after next"). Asking a speaker to "rephrase with one plain date" is a worse failure than it is when typing.
- **Gaps accepted into this brief (Q73 (b), 7 Oct):** French seasons after an elided article ("pour l'hiver", "d'été") and "wintertime" get an answer with no date label until this brief lands.
- **Season and period reproducers (Q74 (a), D84; found in MVP-phone review rounds 17 and 18), all answered with no date label until this brief lands:** "What is on the school lunch menu in summertime?", "Climbing in springtime?", "Lunch menu at Eastertime?", "Menu à la mi-saison ?", "Menu au solstice ?", "Lunch menu at midsummer?", "Menu pour l'hiver ?", "Menu d'été ?", "Lunch menu in wintertime?".
- **Q76 (7 Oct):** every date wording the phone does not read is accepted as a risk moved here, and real failures feed this brief through [[90 System/briefs/P2.7-failure-capture-loop|P2.7]] (David's self-improving loop).
- **Other unread reproducers (Q76 (a), D87; MVP-phone review rounds 18–19):** "Menu le treize ?", "Menu le quatorze ?", "Menu le seize ?" (French day numbers); "Menu pour l'Ascension ?", "Menu à l'Assomption ?", "Menu à l'Épiphanie ?", "Lunch menu on Martinmas?", "Lunch menu on Michaelmas?" (named days); "Menu pour l'été ?", "Menu à l'automne ?" (French seasons after "l'").
- Known cost of (a), to remove here: "November lunch booking deadline?", "What's next?", "Which day is climbing?", "mon menu demain" now ask again.

## Design (proposed; options and trade-offs)
| Option | How | Strength | Weakness | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Keep extending the regexes | More phrases, more review rounds | No new dependency | Round 14 proved it does not converge; cost is paid in review rounds | Rejected |
| **B. Existing date library, fail-closed wrapper (proposed)** | A maintained library (candidates to evaluate: `dateparser`, `parsedatetime`; whether each reads relative dates in English and French is a question the evaluation answers, not an assumption) turns the phrase into a date relative to today in Europe/Brussels. Our wrapper keeps the contracts from MVP-phone: the "(Asked about …)" label is always added, no result or more than one candidate gives `unclear`, no silent rewrite | If the evaluation confirms coverage, it handles the long tail; the guarantees stay ours and testable | A dependency to vet; library defaults can guess (e.g. "Monday" past or future), so the wrapper must pin those settings and tests must prove them | Proposed |
| C. Let the model resolve dates | The model writes an ISO date from the phrase | Could cover wordings a library misses (untested) | Untested here whether the current 4B model resolves dates reliably; its result cannot be checked on its own | Only as a later add-on to B (below) |

**Later, after T9 picks a stronger model (not in this brief's first run):** the model proposes an ISO date from the phrase, and a deterministic check against B's result accepts it only if they agree; disagreement gives `unclear`.
