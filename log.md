## [2026-10-01] ingest | Billing rules and cumulation (Belgian nomenclature)
David asked for the billing rules to be understood and documented, citing "2nd doctor visit same day at 50%". Added `30 Resources/Belgian Healthcare & eHealth/billing-rules-and-cumulation.md` (`public`, draft) from the primary nomenclature PDFs: article 1 and 2 interpretative rules, article 2 text, article 7 (physiotherapy) text, article 8 (nursing) text in force 1.9.2026, article 15 interpretative rules. Source register and glossary extended; hub reading order renumbered.
Finding worth recording: there is no single same-day rule, and the premise needed correcting. Doctors (art. 2) use cumulation *prohibitions*, with no percentage reduction in the article. The real 100%/50% rule is surgery (art. 15 §4), written as fraction notation (`N 600/2`) and conditional on a *distinct operative field* — two incisions in one field are not two fields. Physiotherapy does halve: `560011` M 24 (30 min) vs second-of-day `560696`/`561433` M 12 (15 min), requiring a ≥3-hour gap, indispensability, motivation in the patient file, and revocable by the advising physician. Nursing does *not* reduce: `425014`/`425036`/`425051` (first/second/third+ basic service of the care day) are all W 0,879, and a care-day forfait A/B/C or palliative PA–PC can absorb all per-act billing instead.
Also flagged in the page: general web search on this topic returns mostly French (France) rules that do not apply in Belgium; none were used.

## [2026-10-01] ingest | Full nomenclature corpus and billing-engine rule model
David asked for all the Belgian billing rules, across every healthcare profession, derived from the legal texts for a billing engine. Retrieved the **complete nomenclature**: all 63 article texts plus 45 interpretative-rule documents (~4.9M characters), mined by rule pattern, then written up as 10 new `public` pages — `nomenclature-map.md` (article → profession → key letter, with rule density), `billing-engine-rule-model.md` (19 constraint types with encodings, evaluation order, data model, 18 test cases) and one page per profession: physicians, physiotherapy, nursing, dental, midwifery, speech therapy, labs/imaging, devices/aids.
Structural keystones established from primary text: **the key letter encodes the profession** (art. 1 §2 — N/K doctors, L dental, V midwives, M physio, W nursing, R speech, Z opticians, S audiologists, O orthopaedic technologists, U implants, Q accreditation) and **fee = conventionally-fixed euro value of the key letter × coefficient** (art. 1 §3); **paired code numbers are ambulatory vs hospitalised**, chosen by the patient's status at delivery — except clinical biology, anatomical pathology and in-vitro nuclear medicine, which use the moment the prescription was established.
Beyond the four same-day patterns already recorded, the corpus yielded further percentage rules (candidate specialist at 100%/75% by supervision, art. 1 §4ter; surveillance cumulable only at 50% of value with same-speciality technical acts, art. 25; dental prosthetic milestone percentages, art. 6) and cap scopes that break naive counters: per tooth, per quadrant, per case, per nosological episode, per pregnancy, per prescription, per rolling 24 hours, per device, per audio centre per year, per insured for life, and time-tiered (monthly → quarterly → biannually by month index). Exact temporal definitions captured from art. 26: weekend = Saturday 08:00 to Monday 08:00; public holiday = 21:00 the day before to 08:00 the day after.
One correction made against the current law: an earlier draft cited physiotherapy `560011` as the lead 30-minute code, taken from the text in force since 2014. The current 1 July 2026 text leads with a `567xxx` series (including a new first-session intake code) and keeps `560011` as a §10-limited fallback. Both affected pages corrected, and the lesson recorded in the rule model.
Six articles (27, 28 §1–7, 28 §8, 29, 35, 35bis) had no open-ended current PDF, so versions dating from 2012–2014 were taken; everything drawn from them is flagged as requiring re-check. Largest remaining gap: article 24's complete numbered application-rule list, and the euro value of each key letter.
